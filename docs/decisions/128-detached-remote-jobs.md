- **Decision:** A remote job's harness is launched detached on its device under `setsid` (perl `POSIX::setsid` where a Mac has no `setsid`), not `systemd-run --user`, with its `stdin`, `pid`, `log`, `handoff` and `exit` files in `<workdir_root>/jobs/<job-id>.run/<tag>/`. `orch job run` follows it with short SSH calls, retries an unreachable device with backoff (2 to 60 seconds), and records `run-died` only when the device has a failed exit status for it, confirms the worker gone without one, or gives no certain answer for 30 minutes. The worker tag names the launch (an atomic `mkdir`), so a retried or late launch never starts a second harness; a launch that reaches the device more than 10 minutes after the box sent it is refused. A cancelled run is stopped with SIGTERM, then SIGKILL from 60 seconds on, and followed until the device confirms the worker gone (at most two minutes, after which the slot stays held); the poll that follows a start never fences it on a Mac (Codex PR #129 round 1). On a Mac the stop snapshots the group (each member's pid and start time) while its leader is verified, so a child that outlives the leader's SIGTERM is still known as the job's and killed (round 2). The Mac start also leaves a keeper in the group that ignores SIGTERM and exits only once it is alone, so the group stays provably the job's until it is empty, and a child forked during shutdown is killed as well (round 3).
